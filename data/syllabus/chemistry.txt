## 12.5 Module 5: Equilibrium and Acid Reactions
### 12.5.1 Static and Dynamic Equilibrium
12.5.1.1 conduct practical investigations to analyse the reversibility of chemical reactions, for example: cobalt(II) chloride hydrated and dehydrated; iron(III) nitrate and potassium thiocyanate; burning magnesium; burning steel wool
12.5.1.2 model static and dynamic equilibrium and analyse the differences between open and closed systems
12.5.1.3 analyse examples of non-equilibrium systems in terms of the effect of entropy and enthalpy, for example: combustion reactions; photosynthesis
12.5.1.4 investigate the relationship between collision theory and reaction rate in order to analyse chemical equilibrium reactions
### 12.5.2 Factors that Affect Equilibrium
12.5.2.1 investigate the effects of temperature, concentration, volume and/or pressure on a system at equilibrium and explain how Le Chatelier's principle can be used to predict such effects, for example: heating cobalt(II) chloride hydrate; interaction between nitrogen dioxide and dinitrogen tetroxide; iron(III) thiocyanate and varying concentration of ions
12.5.2.2 explain the overall observations about equilibrium in terms of the collision theory
12.5.2.3 examine how activation energy and heat of reaction affect the position of equilibrium
### 12.5.3 Calculating the Equilibrium Constant (K$_{eq}$)
12.5.3.1 deduce the equilibrium expression (in terms of K$_{eq}$) for homogeneous reactions occurring in solution
12.5.3.2 perform calculations to find the value of K$_{eq}$ and concentrations of substances within an equilibrium system, and use these values to make predictions on the direction in which a reaction may proceed
12.5.3.3 qualitatively analyse the effect of temperature on the value of K$_{eq}$
12.5.3.4 conduct an investigation to determine K$_{eq}$ of a chemical equilibrium system, for example: K$_{eq}$ of the iron(III) thiocyanate equilibrium
12.5.3.5 explore the use of K$_{eq}$ for different types of chemical reactions, including but not limited to: dissociation of ionic solutions; dissociation of acids and bases
### 12.5.4 Solution Equilibria
12.5.4.1 describe and analyse the processes involved in the dissolution of ionic compounds in water
12.5.4.2 investigate the use of solubility equilibria by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples when removing toxicity from foods, for example: toxins in cycad fruit
12.5.4.3 conduct an investigation to determine solubility rules, and predict and analyse the composition of substances when two ionic solutions are mixed, for example: potassium chloride and silver nitrate; potassium iodide and lead nitrate; sodium sulfate and barium nitrate
12.5.4.4 derive equilibrium expressions for saturated solutions in terms of K$_{sp}$ and calculate the solubility of an ionic substance from its K$_{sp}$ value
12.5.4.5 predict the formation of a precipitate given the standard reference values for K$_{sp}$\
## 12.6 Module 6: Acid/Base Reactions
### 12.6.1 Properties of Acids and Bases
12.6.1.1 investigate the correct IUPAC nomenclature and properties of common inorganic acids and bases
12.6.1.2 conduct an investigation to demonstrate the preparation and use of indicators as illustrators of the characteristics and properties of acids and bases and their reversible reactions
12.6.1.3 predict the products of acid reactions and write balanced equations to represent: acids and bases; acids and carbonates; acids and metals
12.6.1.4 investigate applications of neutralisation reactions in everyday life and industrial processes
12.6.1.5 conduct a practical investigation to measure the enthalpy of neutralisation
12.6.1.6 explore the changes in definitions and models of an acid and a base over time to explain the limitations of each model, including but not limited to: Arrhenius' theory; Brønsted–Lowry theory
### 12.6.2 Using Brønsted–Lowry Theory
12.6.2.1 conduct a practical investigation to measure the pH of a range of acids and bases
12.6.2.2 calculate pH, pOH, hydrogen ion concentration ([H$^{+}$]) and hydroxide ion concentration ([OH$^{–}$]) for a range of solutions
12.6.2.3 conduct an investigation to demonstrate the use of pH to indicate the differences between the strength of acids and bases
12.6.2.4 write ionic equations to represent the dissociation of acids and bases in water, conjugate acid/base pairs in solution and amphiprotic nature of some salts, for example: sodium hydrogen carbonate; potassium dihydrogen phosphate
12.6.2.5 construct models and/or animations to communicate the differences between strong, weak, concentrated and dilute acids and bases
12.6.2.6 calculate the pH of the resultant solution when solutions of acids and/or bases are diluted or mixed
### 12.6.3 Quantitative Analysis
12.6.3.1 conduct practical investigations to analyse the concentration of an unknown acid or base by titration
12.6.3.2 investigate titration curves and conductivity graphs to analyse data to indicate characteristic reaction profiles, for example: strong acid/strong base; strong acid/weak base; weak acid/strong base
12.6.3.3 model neutralisation of strong and weak acids and bases using a variety of media
12.6.3.4 calculate and apply the dissociation constant (K$_{a}$) and pK$_{a}$ (pK$_{a}$ = -log$_{10}$ (K$_{a}$)) to determine the difference between strong and weak acids
12.6.3.5 explore acid/base analysis techniques that are applied: in industries; by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples; using digital probes and instruments
12.6.3.6 conduct a chemical analysis of a common household substance for its acidity or basicity , for example: soft drink; wine; juice; medicine
12.6.3.7 conduct a practical investigation to prepare a buffer and demonstrate its properties
12.6.3.8 describe the importance of buffers in natural systems
## 12.7 Module 7: Organic Chemistry
### 12.7.1 Nomenclature
12.7.1.1 investigate the nomenclature of organic chemicals, up to C8, using IUPAC conventions, including simple methyl and ethyl branched chains, including: alkanes; alkenes; alkynes; alcohols (primary, secondary and tertiary); aldehydes and ketones; carboxylic acids; amines and amides; halogenated organic compounds
12.7.1.2 explore and distinguish the different types of structural isomers, including saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons, including: chain isomers; position isomers; functional group isomers
### 12.7.2 Hydrocarbons
12.7.2.1 construct models, identify the functional group, and write structural and molecular formulae for homologous series of organic chemical compounds, up to C8 : alkanes; alkenes; alkynes
12.7.2.2 conduct an investigation to compare the properties of organic chemical compounds within a homologous series, and explain these differences in terms of bonding
12.7.2.3 analyse the shape of molecules formed between carbon atoms when a single, double or triple bond is formed between them
12.7.2.4 explain the properties within and between the homologous series of alkanes with reference to the intermolecular and intramolecular bonding present
12.7.2.5 describe the procedures required to safely handle and dispose of organic substances
12.7.2.6 examine the environmental, economic and sociocultural implications of obtaining and using hydrocarbons from the Earth
### 12.7.3 Products of Reactions Involving Hydrocarbons
12.7.3.1 investigate, write equations and construct models to represent the reactions of unsaturated hydrocarbons when added to a range of chemicals, including but not limited to: hydrogen (H$_{2}$); halogens (X$_{2}$); hydrogen halides (HX); water (H$_{2}$O)
12.7.3.2 investigate, write equations and construct models to represent the reactions of saturated hydrocarbons when substituted with halogens
### 12.7.4 Alcohols
12.7.4.1 investigate the structural formulae, properties and functional group including: primary; secondary; tertiary alcohols
12.7.4.2 explain the properties within and between the homologous series of alcohols with reference to the intermolecular and intramolecular bonding present
12.7.4.3 conduct a practical investigation to measure and reliably compare the enthalpy of combustion for a range of alcohols
12.7.4.4 write equations, state conditions and predict products to represent the reactions of alcohols, including but not limited to: combustion; dehydration; substitution with HX; oxidation
12.7.4.5 investigate the production of alcohols, including: substitution reactions of halogenated organic compounds; fermentation
12.7.4.6 investigate the products of the oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols
12.7.4.7 compare and contrast fuels from organic sources to biofuels, including ethanol
### 12.7.5 Reactions of Organic Acids and Bases
12.7.5.1 investigate the structural formulae, properties and functional group including: primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols; aldehydes and ketones; amines and amides; carboxylic acids
12.7.5.2 explain the properties within and between the homologous series of carboxylic acids amines and amides with reference to the intermolecular and intramolecular bonding present
12.7.5.3 investigate the production, in a school laboratory, of simple esters
12.7.5.4 investigate the differences between an organic acid and organic base
12.7.5.5 investigate the structure and action of soaps and detergents
12.7.5.6 draft and construct flow charts to show reaction pathways for chemical synthesis, including those that involve more than one step
### 12.7.6 Polymers
12.7.6.1 model and compare the structure, properties and uses of addition polymers of ethylene and related monomers, for example: polyethylene (PE); polyvinyl chloride (PVC); polystyrene (PS); polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)
12.7.6.2 model and compare the structure, properties and uses of condensation polymers, for example: nylon; polyesters
## 12.8 Module 8: Applying Chemical Ideas
### 12.8.1 Analysis of Inorganic Substances
12.8.1.1 analyse the need for monitoring the environment
12.8.1.2 conduct qualitative investigations – using flame tests, precipitation and complexation reactions as appropriate – to test for the presence in aqueous solution of the following ions: cations: barium (Ba$^{2+}$), calcium (Ca$^{2+}$), magnesium (Mg$^{2+}$), lead(II) (Pb$^{2+}$), silver ion (Ag$^{+}$), copper(II) (Cu$^{2+}$), iron(II) (Fe$^{2+}$), iron(III) (Fe$^{3+}$); anions: chloride (Cl$^{–}$), bromide (Br$^{–}$), iodide (I$^{–}$), hydroxide (OH$^{–}$), acetate (CH$_{3}$COO$^{–}$), carbonate (CO$_{3}$$^{2–}$), sulfate (SO$_{4}$$^{2–}$), phosphate (PO$_{4}$$^{3–}$)
12.8.1.3 conduct investigations and/or process data involving: gravimetric analysis; precipitation titrations
12.8.1.4 conduct investigations and/or process data to determine the concentration of coloured species and/or metal ions in aqueous solution, including but not limited to, the use of: colourimetry; ultraviolet‑visible spectrophotometry; atomic absorption spectroscopy
### 12.8.2 Analysis of Organic Substances
12.8.2.1 conduct qualitative investigations to test for the presence in organic molecules of the following functional groups: carbon–carbon double bonds; hydroxyl groups; carboxylic acids
12.8.2.2 investigate the processes used to analyse the structure of simple organic compounds addressed in the course, including but not limited to: proton and carbon-13 NMR; mass spectrometry; infrared spectroscopy
### 12.8.3 Chemical Synthesis and Design
12.8.3.1 evaluate the factors that need to be considered when designing a chemical synthesis process, including but not limited to: availability of reagents; reaction conditions; yield and purity; industrial uses (eg pharmaceutical, cosmetics, cleaning products, fuels); environmental, social and economic issues
